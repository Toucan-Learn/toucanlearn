"Present to me about one country that spawned since the end of WW1. Tell me 3 important details!"

Political structure and governance. Yugoslavia was formed after the fallout of World War One in 1918. Up to World War Two, it ran under a monarchy until it was invaded by the Axis powers. After the war, the country transitioned to a communist structure under Josip Broz Tito. The federation consisted of six republics: Slovenia, Croatia, Bosnia and Herzegovina, Montenegro, Serbia and Macedonia.

Breakup and conflicts. Tito died in 1980, and his death led to increased nationalism and tensions among the republics. A sequence of violent conflicts, known as the Yugoslav Wars, eventually led to the split of Yugoslavia. Ethnic tensions defined these wars and resulted in major population displacement.

Legacy. Yugoslavia was a unique experiment in multi-ethnic governance that ultimately faced serious challenges from nationalistic sentiment. Its dissolution created several independent states, each with its own national identity and political challenges. That history still influences the politics and societies of the Balkan region today.

Origin story · anonymised

The learner who built the method.

One of the first learners Josh supported was a young autistic learner with a PDA profile. He was exceptionally bright, frequently dysregulated and spending much of the school day outside classrooms. School had become almost impossible for him to access.

The question was not “How do I stop this behaviour?” It was “How do I make school somewhere he actually wants to be?”

01 · Relationship Make the day tolerable first

No constant correction, forced worksheets or power struggles. The first goal was simply: let’s get through today together.

02 · Interests Sports and music became the curriculum

They were not rewards held back until work was finished. They were genuine routes into trust, conversation, thinking and learning.

03 · A different route The English task became a song

He created the ideas through music and conversation while Josh acted as scribe. The learning objective stayed the same; only the route changed.

04 · Hidden competence The penhold breakthrough

The grip he used to teach Josh table tennis already contained the motor control needed to hold a pencil. The skill existed; it only needed transferring.

Signature moment

“You already know how.”

Writing was described as impossible, yet the learner had taught Josh a precise penhold grip months earlier. Connecting the two did not teach a new movement. It helped him recognise an ability he already possessed.

The music room

Creativity was never the barrier

They headed to the drums and guitars because he wanted to play. Later, an untouched English task became songwriting: ideas, improvised lines and laughter, with Josh writing as he created. The barrier was transcription—not imagination or intelligence.

Relationship before curriculum Trust before challenge Interests before compliance Adapt the route, not the expectation Behaviour communicates unmet needs Look for hidden competence Build self-belief through real success Let success create motivation

Toucan Learn did not begin with a business plan. It began with one learner, one table-tennis bat and the belief that ability often appears when we change the route instead of lowering the destination.
